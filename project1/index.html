How solar panels turns sunlight into electricity

By Dr. Wangari Njeri Published 20 august 2026 . 6 min read

Introduction

Every hour, the Sun delivers more energy to Earth than the whole world uses in a year. Solar energy is the light and heat that comes from the Sun. Countries near the equator, like Kenya, receive about 5 to 7 kWh of sunlight per square metre every day. [1]

The photovoltaic effect

Solar panels are made of PV cells, usually made from silicon (Si). When photons (particles of light) hit the cell, they knock electrons loose. This flow of electrons is an electric current.

The energy of a photon is given by the formula:

E = h × f

where h is Planck's constant (6.63 × 10−34 J·s) and f is the frequency of the light.

steps in the process

  • 1. Sunlight hits the solar cell.< /li>
  • Electrons are knocked free from silicon atoms.
  • 3. An electric field pushes the electrons in one direction.
  • 4. Metal contacts collect the electrons as DC electricity.
  • 5. An inverter changes it to AC for home use.

    • Why It Matters for the Climate

    Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide (CO2) and methane (CH4). A typical home solar system can prevent about 1.5 tonnes of CO2 each year. As one engineer put it, “the cleanest energy is the energy that falls on your roof for free”.

    Solar power is now the cheapest source of electricity in history.

    — World Energy Outlook Report

    Did you know?

    The first practical silicon solar cell was made in 1954 at Bell Labs. It had an efficiency of only 6%. Modern panels reach over 22%.
